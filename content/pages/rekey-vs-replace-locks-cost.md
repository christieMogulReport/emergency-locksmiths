**Short version:** if your lock still works and you just need **old keys to stop working** (lost keys, a move, a roommate or ex who kept a copy), **rekey it**. If the lock is **damaged, worn out, or forced in a break-in**, or you want a different kind of lock (a deadbolt, a smart lock, a high-security cylinder), **replace it**. Published guides put rekeying at roughly **$50 to $130 per lock**, or **$100 to $300 per door once the trip fee is counted**, and replacement at roughly **$80 to $350 per lock**, with one guide's average at **about $315**. Nights, weekends, and holidays cost more.

Every number below comes from a published source, linked next to it. They are national figures, not quotes for your city, so call to confirm. If you're locked out and someone (a child, a vulnerable adult, a pet) is inside, **call 911 first**.

## What's the difference?

- **Rekeying** keeps your existing lock and changes the pins inside it so a new key works and the old keys don't. You pay mostly for labor, not parts ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm), [HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)).
- **Replacing** removes the whole lockset (handle or knob and all the hardware inside) and installs a new one, with new keys ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)). You pay for the hardware and the labor.

Both leave you with a lock the old keys can't open. The question is whether the lock itself is still worth keeping.

## Rekey or replace? A quick decision guide

| Your situation | Usually | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lost your keys, or they were stolen | **Rekey** | The lock is fine; the problem is who has a key. Angi and HomeGuide both list lost keys as a reason to rekey. See [lost your keys: should you rekey?](/lost-keys-should-you-rekey/) |
| Just moved into a home or apartment | **Rekey** (or replace if the locks are worn) | You can't know how many copies the last owner handed out ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)). Renters: check your lease and ask the landlord first. |
| Roommate, ex, cleaner, or sitter still has a key | **Rekey** | Angi lists changing service providers who hold a key as a reason to rekey ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm)). |
| Lock was forced, pried, or drilled in a break-in | **Replace** the damaged lock | HomeGuide says replacement is the better choice when a lock is "old, damaged, or no longer functioning properly." Angi says to rekey immediately after a break-in, and to act the same way if a lock is damaged. |
| Keys stick, the lock is rusty or chipped | **Replace** | Angi's signs that it's time for a new lock: keys that stick even after lubrication, rust, broken or chipped parts ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm)). |
| You want a deadbolt, a smart lock, or a higher-security lock | **Replace** | That's new hardware, not new pins ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)). |
| You want one key for every door | **Rekey**, if the locks share a keyway | Locks of the same brand or keyhole type can usually be rekeyed to one key; if they're mixed brands and types, Angi says you'll need a locksmith to set up a master key system ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm), [HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)). |

**About the sources.** Angi's two articles don't agree with each other exactly (one gives an average replacement of $150, the other about $315), which is a good reminder that these are broad ranges. HomeGuide's rekey figure looks higher than its replacement figure only because it folds in the service call. The Locksmith Ledger figures are averages from a trade magazine's survey of working locksmiths, which it says is "not a scientific poll" and that prices are generally higher in big cities.

**How to read it:** for one door during business hours, rekeying and a basic replacement can come out close, because the trip fee is most of the bill. The gap opens when you have several doors (rekeying reuses the hardware you already own) or when you want a better lock (the hardware is the expensive part). That's our reading of the sources, not a quote.

## Does it have to be tonight?

- **Lost keys with your address on them, or a break-in:** treat it as urgent. Angi says to rekey immediately after a break-in or a missing key ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm)). If a forced door won't lock at all, the emergency call is worth it.
- **Moved in, roommate left, keys just misplaced at home:** usually not an emergency. HomeGuide and Angi both suggest booking non-urgent lock work in regular hours to avoid the emergency rate, which HomeGuide puts at roughly double the normal service call ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks), [Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/change-door-locks-cost.htm)).
- **Already paying for a lockout visit?** Ask for the rekey price while the locksmith is there. Bundling jobs into one visit means one trip fee ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)).

## Can you rekey it yourself?

Sometimes. **Kwikset SmartKey** locks can be rekeyed by the owner in a few steps with the SmartKey tool that comes in the box, but only on Kwikset locks, and **you need the current working key**; if it's lost or stolen, Kwikset tells you to contact a builder, a locksmith, or its support team ([Kwikset](https://www.kwikset.com/smartkey-security), [Kwikset support](https://www.kwikset.com/support/answers/how-to-re-key-your-smartkey-security-locks)). For other locks, Angi notes brand-specific rekey kits cost about $10 to $25, but picking the wrong one can leave you with mismatched pins and keys that don't work ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm)). Don't experiment on your only working door at midnight.

## Don't get talked into a lock you don't need

Angi's writer notes that "some locksmiths may tell you that you should change your locks," but in many cases you can just rekey them ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm)). In an emergency that matters more: the BBB warns that locksmith scammers often insist a lock can't be picked and must be drilled and replaced with an expensive new one, which it says is most likely a deception ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)).

Before anyone starts work, ask:

- "Is the lock damaged, or can you **rekey** it instead of replacing it?"
- "What's the **total**, including the trip fee, labor, parts, and any after-hours charge?"
- "If it has to be replaced, which lock are you installing, and what does it cost? Can I buy my own?" (HomeGuide says buying your own hardware can save 10% to 30%; [HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks).)
- "Can you rekey all my doors to the same key in this visit?"

Then **pay by card** and get an itemized invoice with the business name and address. For the full set of checks, see [How to tell if a locksmith is a scam](/how-to-tell-if-locksmith-is-scam-checklist/), and for prices on every other job, the [locksmith price list](/locksmith-cost-price-list/).

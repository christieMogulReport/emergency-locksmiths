**Short version:** the best place to find a 24-hour locksmith is **not the first search result at 2 a.m.** In order, try: a locksmith you saved earlier or one a friend has used; **roadside assistance** if it's a car; your **state's list of licensed locksmiths** (in the states that keep one); the trade association's **ALOA "Find a Locksmith"** directory; and only then a web search, read carefully. Whoever you find, spend five minutes checking that the **business name, street address, licence, and vehicle** all match before anyone comes to your door. The state licence lookups are linked in a table below.

If a child, a vulnerable adult, or a pet is locked inside a house or car, **call 911 first**. This page is about *finding* someone. For the step-by-step checks on the phone, at the door, and when paying, use our [locksmith scam checklist](/how-to-tell-if-locksmith-is-scam-checklist/), and for fair prices see the [locksmith price list](/locksmith-cost-price-list/).

## Why "locksmith near me" is a risky search

State regulators keep warning about the same thing. Alabama's locksmith licensing board says that when you're locked out you'll probably search on your phone, and reminds you that **not all locksmiths listed online are actually in your area, not all are licensed by the state, and not all will charge the quoted amount** once they're on site ([Alabama Electronic Security Board of Licensure](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/consumers/consumer-information/)). Maryland's licensing program warns that some locksmiths advertising online "may not be licensed," may lack training, and may "use intimidating tactics to overcharge you" ([Maryland Department of Labor](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/locksmiths/lockadvisory.shtml)). Connecticut's consumer protection department adds that fraudulent businesses pay for keywords so they appear at the top of search results ([CT DCP](https://portal.ct.gov/dcp/news-releases-from-the-department-of-consumer-protection/2026-news-releases/be-wary-of-scammers-posing-as-locksmith-technicians)).

So the goal is to start somewhere that has already done some checking for you, and then check the rest yourself.

## Where to look, best first

### 1. A locksmith you (or someone you trust) already know

Both Maryland and Alabama tell consumers to **pick a locksmith before there's an emergency**. Maryland suggests asking family or friends for a locksmith they've used, and calling a few locksmiths to ask for their business street address so you know whether each is mobile, home-based, or a storefront ([Maryland Department of Labor](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/locksmiths/lockadvisory.shtml)). Alabama's board recommends finding a licensed local locksmith ahead of time and saving their information in your phone ([AESBL](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/consumers/consumer-information/)). The FTC has given the same tip ([FTC consumer alert, reproduced](https://rockymountainsecuritygroup.com/files/HIRE_A_LOCKSMITH.pdf)).

If you're reading this calmly, do it now: find one or two, check them with the routine below, and save them in your phone as "Locksmith (checked)."

### 2. Locked out of a car? Roadside assistance first

Maryland's advice for a car lockout is to contact AAA, the roadside assistance in your purchase or lease contract, or your car insurer's roadside service **first** ([Maryland Department of Labor](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/locksmiths/lockadvisory.shtml)). The FTC notes these services use pre-approved companies ([FTC alert](https://rockymountainsecuritygroup.com/files/HIRE_A_LOCKSMITH.pdf)). Check your insurance app, car maker's app, or the back of your insurance card before searching.

### 3. Your state's list of licensed locksmiths

In states that license locksmiths, the state's lookup is the closest thing to an official directory. Alabama says **all licensed locksmiths can be found on its website** under Licensees ([AESBL](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/consumers/consumer-information/)), and Maryland says every approved employee of a licensed locksmith appears under that licensee in its license search ([Maryland Department of Labor](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/locksmiths/lockadvisory.shtml)). Many state lookups let you search by city or ZIP as well as by name. See the state-by-state table further down this page.

A licence isn't a price guarantee, but it means the state has a record of who the business is, and in most licensing states a background check was part of getting it (California, for example, requires a state DOJ and FBI criminal history check for locksmith company applicants, per the [California Bureau of Security and Investigative Services](https://www.bsis.ca.gov/forms_pubs/locksmith_fact.shtml)). It also gives you somewhere to complain.

### 4. ALOA's "Find a Locksmith" directory

[FindALocksmith.com](https://www.findalocksmith.com/findalocksmith.html) is run by ALOA, the locksmith trade association, and lets you search by postal code, city, or state. **Everyone listed is an ALOA member**, and ALOA says each membership applicant has to be "cleared and vetted by existing members" and agree to professional and ethical standards ([Find a Locksmith](https://findalocksmith.com/), [About Find a Locksmith](https://findalocksmith.com/about.html)).

Two things to know: membership is voluntary, so plenty of honest locksmiths aren't listed (especially in rural areas); and members can pay a monthly fee to be a "Featured Locksmith" with preferred placement in the results ([For Locksmiths](https://www.findalocksmith.com/for-locksmiths.html)). A featured spot is an ad, not a ranking. And check whether a listing says it offers 24-hour or emergency service, then confirm that on the phone.

### 5. A web or map search, read carefully

If you have to search, a few things help:

- **Know what Google's "Google Verified" badge means.** Locksmith ads on Google in the U.S. are restricted: to advertise, a locksmith must pass Google's **Advanced Verification**, which reviews public records such as state registrations and professional licences, includes a recorded video interview, and checks for "evidence of unauthorized or misleading business practices" ([Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13527633?hl=en), [Advanced Verification policies](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7167922?hl=en)). Google treats locksmiths as an "urgent" category in its Local Services Ads, with **background checks** on top of that, and lists a state-level business licence among the requirements ([Google Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778?co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS&hl=En)). Advertisers who pass get the "Google Verified" badge ([Google](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/16498018?hl=en)).
- **But the badge is not a money-back promise.** Google has discontinued the old "Google Guarantee" money-back guarantee; the deadline to claim reimbursement for services booked through Local Services Ads was December 7, 2025 ([Google](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/16498018?hl=en)). Verification also doesn't fix the price. You still need a total quote.
- **Ignore the star rating on its own.** A college student in one Reddit story was asked for $600 by a company with hundreds of near-perfect reviews (details on our [scam checklist](/how-to-tell-if-locksmith-is-scam-checklist/)). Connecticut DCP's tips for spotting fake reviews: lots of reviews in a short time, reviewers with only one review, and vague five-star reviews with no detail ([CT DCP](https://portal.ct.gov/dcp/news-releases-from-the-department-of-consumer-protection/2026-news-releases/be-wary-of-scammers-posing-as-locksmith-technicians)).
- **Don't call a number from a sticker on your door** without looking the company up. In 2026, police in Tukwila, Washington, warned of fake locksmith stickers placed over real ones on business doors ([KOMO News](https://komonews.com/news/local/tukwila-locksmith-scam-legitimate-stickers-removed-doors-commercial-businesses-replaced-fakes-inflated-prices-copy-key-burglary-financial-information-fraud)).

## The five-minute verification routine

Whatever route you took, run these checks before you agree to anything. Most take a minute on your phone.

1. **Get the exact business name and street address.** ALOA's advice: if the legal business name and location aren't stated, ask; "if there's hesitation, this could be a cause for concern. Hang up and move on" ([Find a Locksmith](https://www.findalocksmith.com/findalocksmith.html)). Some honest locksmiths are mobile-only, which is fine if they say so plainly ([Maryland Department of Labor](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/locksmiths/lockadvisory.shtml)).
2. **Check the address on a map.** Street view should show a real business or a plausible home base, not an empty lot or an unrelated shop ([CT DCP](https://portal.ct.gov/dcp/news-releases-from-the-department-of-consumer-protection/2026-news-releases/be-wary-of-scammers-posing-as-locksmith-technicians)). Alabama suggests simply asking, "Where in the state are you located?" ([AESBL](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/consumers/consumer-information/)).
3. **Look up the licence** if your state issues one (table below). Ask for the number on the phone. Search by the business name too, because a real licence number can be borrowed.
4. **Search the business name plus "scam" or "complaint,"** and check the BBB, as Connecticut DCP suggests ([CT DCP](https://portal.ct.gov/dcp/news-releases-from-the-department-of-consumer-protection/2026-news-releases/be-wary-of-scammers-posing-as-locksmith-technicians)). Several differently named listings sharing one phone number is a warning sign.
5. **Get a total price for your exact job.** For a car, give the make and model and ask for "as firm a quote as possible," plus whether there's a trip charge, as Alabama's board puts it ([AESBL](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/consumers/consumer-information/)). Ask what you'd owe if you turn them away at the door because the price changed ([AESBL](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/consumers/consumer-information/), [Find a Locksmith](https://www.findalocksmith.com/findalocksmith.html)).
6. **Ask who's coming and in what.** Get the technician's name and vehicle. Service vehicles should be clearly marked with the company name, or the technician should show company ID ([Find a Locksmith](https://www.findalocksmith.com/findalocksmith.html)). In licensing states, ask to see the state-issued card: Maryland requires a photo ID card showing the licence number ([Maryland Department of Labor](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/locksmiths/lockadvisory.shtml)), and Alabama's board says to ask to see the ID badge it issues and not to let locksmith personnel in unless they are currently licensed ([AESBL](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/consumers/consumer-information/), [AESBL FAQ](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/faq/how-can-i-be-sure-a-company-or-individual-is-licensed/)).

Expect the locksmith to check you too. A legitimate locksmith should ask for proof of ID and that you're allowed into the home or car ([Find a Locksmith](https://www.findalocksmith.com/findalocksmith.html), [Maryland Department of Labor](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/locksmiths/lockadvisory.shtml)). Maryland also suggests asking a family member, friend, or trusted neighbor to meet you and the locksmith ([Maryland Department of Labor](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/locksmiths/lockadvisory.shtml)).

## State licence lookups

ALOA lists **13 states** that require locksmith licensing ([ALOA advocacy](https://www.aloa.org/advocacy)). Here is where each one lets the public check a locksmith. Agencies move their search pages from time to time; if a link breaks, search the agency name plus "license lookup," and always use the official .gov site rather than a third-party "verification" page.

| State | Who licenses locksmiths | Where to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alabama | Alabama Electronic Security Board of Licensure | [Licensees list](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/licensees/) ([board FAQ](https://aesbl.alabama.gov/faq/how-can-i-be-sure-a-company-or-individual-is-licensed/)) |
| California | Bureau of Security and Investigative Services (BSIS), Dept. of Consumer Affairs | [BSIS Verify a License](https://www.bsis.ca.gov/forms_pubs/online_services/verify_license) or the [DCA License Search](https://search.dca.ca.gov/) |
| Connecticut | Department of Consumer Protection (each individual locksmith must be registered) | Search the technician's name on [eLicense.ct.gov](https://www.elicense.ct.gov/) ([CT DCP](https://portal.ct.gov/dcp/news-releases-from-the-department-of-consumer-protection/2026-news-releases/be-wary-of-scammers-posing-as-locksmith-technicians)) |
| Illinois | Dept. of Financial and Professional Regulation (IDFPR) | [IDFPR License Lookup](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/checklicense.html) ([IDFPR locksmith page](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/profs/lock.html)). ALOA reports the law is set to sunset Jan. 1, 2029 ([ALOA](https://www.aloa.org/advocacy)). |
| Louisiana | Office of State Fire Marshal (OSFM) | OSFM's online licence records at [lasfm.org](https://www.lasfm.org/) ([Louisiana Locksmith & Security Association](https://www.llssa.org/how-to-verify-your-locksmiths-license)) |
| Maryland | Maryland Locksmiths Licensing Program, Dept. of Labor | [Locksmith licence search](https://www.dllr.state.md.us/cgi-bin/ElectronicLicensing/OP_search/OP_search.cgi?calling_app=LOCKSMITH%3A%3ALOCKSMITH_qselect) (all [Maryland licensing queries](https://labor.maryland.gov/pq/)) |
| Nevada | County sheriff where the business is based (state law requires a permit; renewed every 5 years) | Ask for the permit and check with that county sheriff's office ([NRS 655.070](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-655.html)) |
| New Jersey | Fire Alarm, Burglar Alarm and Locksmith Advisory Committee, Division of Consumer Affairs | [NJ licence verification](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/fbl/Pages/verification.aspx) |
| North Carolina | North Carolina Locksmith Licensing Board | [Verify License](https://nclocksmithboard.org/verify-license/) (type the name only, leave "Credential" blank, per the board) |
| Oklahoma | Oklahoma Department of Labor (Alarm, Locksmith, and Fire Sprinkler program) | [Verify a company or individual licence](https://oklahoma.gov/labor/safety-and-health/safety-standards/alarm-locksmith-fire-sprinkler-service/licensing-requirements.html) (the lists showed licences valid as of Oct. 29, 2026 when we checked) |
| Oregon | Construction Contractors Board (locksmith certification) | [Oregon Certified Locksmith Search](https://search.ccb.state.or.us/search/ocls_search.asp) ([CCB](https://www.oregon.gov/ccb/pages/specialty%20licenses.aspx)) |
| Texas | Department of Public Safety, Private Security Program | [TOPS licence search](https://tops.portal.texas.gov/psp-self-service/search/index) ([Texas DPS](https://www.dps.texas.gov/section/private-security/private-security)) |
| Virginia | Department of Criminal Justice Services (DCJS) | [DCJS credential search](https://www.dcjs.virginia.gov/division-licensure-and-regulatory-services/external-verification-tool) |

**A few state details worth knowing:**

- **Oregon** requires the certificate even if a locksmith holds a licence from another state, but exempts landlords, property managers, and tow truck operators working within their roles, among others ([Oregon CCB](https://www.oregon.gov/ccb/pages/specialty%20licenses.aspx)).
- **Virginia** counts anyone who advertises lock skills as a locksmith even under another label, such as "lock technician," "unlocking technician," or "lock opener" ([Virginia DCJS](https://www.dcjs.virginia.gov/licensure-and-regulatory-affairs/locksmith)).
- **Maryland** requires general liability insurance for all licensees and says the itemized invoice should include the locksmith licence number ([Maryland Department of Labor](https://labor.maryland.gov/license/locksmiths/lockadvisory.shtml)).
- Some **cities and counties** license locksmiths where the state doesn't; New York City and Nassau County are examples ([Intellidrive state-by-state table](https://intellidriveos.com/locksmith-license)).

**If your state isn't on the list,** there's no licence to look up, and "licensed and bonded" in an ad may not mean much. Lean harder on the business name, street address, ALOA membership, complaint searches, and a firm total price.

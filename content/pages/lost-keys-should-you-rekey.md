**Short version:** the danger of lost keys depends less on the keys than on **what was with them**. A bare key ring dropped across town is mostly an inconvenience. Keys lost with **your address on the tag**, in a **wallet or bag with your ID or mail**, **near your home**, or **stolen** rather than dropped should be treated as working keys in a stranger's hands: **rekey every lock they fit, today or tomorrow**. Published guides put rekeying at roughly **$50 to $130 per lock**, **$100 to $300 per door once the trip fee is counted**, and **$200 to $500 for a whole home**. If you need someone tonight, the **emergency** call-out alone runs roughly **$130 to $240**. If a garage remote was on the ring, **erase the opener's memory** too.

Every number below comes from a published source, linked next to it. They are national figures, not quotes for your city, so call to confirm. If you're locked out and someone (a child, a vulnerable adult, a pet) is inside, **call 911 first**.

## The one question that decides it

A key works because its cuts match the pins in one lock, and nothing on the metal says which door that is. So the real question is: **could someone who finds these keys connect them to your home?** Police agencies give the same advice from the other side. Tasmania Police says not to put your full name and address on key tags because "if you lose them, this could allow a stranger to easily identify your home" ([Tasmania Police](https://www.police.tas.gov.au/home-residential-safety-tips/)), and Johns Hopkins Public Safety warns that "lost keys can lead a thief to your car or home" ([JHU Public Safety](https://publicsafety.jhu.edu/resources-and-services/crime-prevention-education/)).

| What went missing | What a finder knows | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keys with an **address tag** or your name | The exact door to try | **Rekey today** (tonight if you can't secure the home otherwise) |
| Keys in a **wallet, purse, or bag** with your ID, mail, or car registration | Your address and your name | **Rekey today**, then deal with the cards separately |
| Keys **stolen** (bag snatched, car broken into, taken in a burglary) | Possibly your address, and someone chose to take them | **Rekey today**. See [rekeying after a break-in](/rekeying-locks-after-break-in/) if your home was entered |
| Keys lost **near home** (your street, building hallway, parking lot) | A short list of nearby doors | **Rekey soon**, within a day or two |
| A **plain key ring** with no tags, lost far from home | Nothing that points to your door | Retrace your steps; a copy from your spare may do. **Rekey if they stay lost** and it bothers you |

This is our reading of the police advice above and common locksmith guidance, not a rule. If you're unsure which row you're in, treat it as the riskier one: a rekey costs less than most emergency call-outs.

## What to do in the first hour

1. **Retrace your steps once, properly.** Check pockets, the car, and the last places you stopped. Call those places and leave a phone number.
2. **Make sure you can get in.** If you can't, that's a lockout, not a rekey. See [locked out of your house at night](/locked-out-of-house-at-night-cost/) for what that costs and who to call. A spare key with a trusted neighbor or relative is the cheapest fix; Tasmania Police advises leaving spares with someone you trust rather than hiding one outside ([Tasmania Police](https://www.police.tas.gov.au/home-residential-safety-tips/)).
3. **List every lock the lost keys fit.** Front, back, side, the door from the garage into the house, a shed or gate padlock, a mailbox. Rekeying the front door and forgetting the back one leaves the old key working.
4. **Lock up from inside** until the locks are rekeyed, using deadbolts and window locks on doors you're not using.
5. **Garage remote on the ring? Erase it.** On Chamberlain and LiftMaster openers, Chamberlain's instructions are to press and hold the **Learn** button on the motor unit for about **six seconds**, until the light goes out; that erases every programmed remote and keypad code, and you then reprogram the ones you still have ([Chamberlain support](https://support.chamberlaingroup.com/s/article/Garage-Door-Activates-On-Its-Own), [Chamberlain video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hlcZxkAXdcs)). Other brands differ, so check your manual.
6. **Renters:** call your landlord or property manager before booking anyone. They may need to approve, or handle, a lock change, and a key to a shared building door is their problem to fix. Check your lease.

## Rekey, replace, or just cut a copy?

- **Rekey** when the locks are fine and you only need the lost keys to stop working. The locksmith changes the pins inside your existing locks; HomeGuide lists losing a set of keys as one of the common reasons to rekey, and notes a locksmith can rekey several doors to work with **one key** ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)). Angi's advice: if a house key has gone missing, rekey the locks ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm)).
- **Replace** only if the locks were already worn, weak, or due for an upgrade (a better deadbolt, a smart lock). Losing a key isn't, by itself, a reason to buy new hardware.
- **Just cut a copy** from your spare when the keys were plain, lost far away, and nothing linked them to you. Angi puts key duplication at **$2 to $20 per key** ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/keys-home-locksmith-assistance-and-costs.htm)).

Full side-by-side: [Rekey vs. replace locks](/rekey-vs-replace-locks-cost/).

## Tonight or tomorrow morning?

- **Call tonight** if your address or ID was with the keys, the keys were stolen, or you can't secure every door from inside. Those are the cases where waiting makes little sense.
- **Tomorrow is usually fine** if the keys were plain, lost away from home, and every door locks from inside. A daytime visit avoids the emergency call-out, which HomeGuide puts at roughly double the normal one ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)). HomeGuide counts holiday calls as emergency service, so with Thanksgiving this week, ask before you book ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)).
- **Book every lock in one visit**, keyed to one new key, so you pay a single trip fee ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)).

## Don't let a lost key turn into a $400 lock job

People who've just lost their keys are in a hurry and searching "locksmith near me" on a phone, which is exactly where bait-and-switch outfits advertise. The Minnesota Attorney General warns of out-of-state call centers posing as local locksmiths that "significantly jacked up the price" once on site ([Minnesota AG](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/consumer/publications/hiringalocksmith.asp)). The BBB says scammers may insist a lock must be drilled and replaced with an expensive new one, and notes it isn't routine for a locksmith to insist on drilling ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)).

A rekey after lost keys should never need drilling, because nothing is wrong with the lock. Before anyone comes:

- Ask for the **business name and location**. Minnesota's AG says not to use a company that won't give one, and to beware of companies that answer the phone with just "locksmith" ([Minnesota AG](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/consumer/publications/hiringalocksmith.asp)).
- Say "**rekey**, not replace," give the number of locks and the brand if you know it, and ask for the **total**: trip fee, per-lock labor, new keys, and any after-hours charge.
- If the on-site price doesn't match the phone quote, the BBB's advice is to have someone else do the job ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)).
- **Never sign a blank work order**, get an **itemized receipt**, and **pay by card** ([Minnesota AG](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/consumer/publications/hiringalocksmith.asp), [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)).

For the full list of red flags, see [How to tell if a locksmith is a scam](/how-to-tell-if-locksmith-is-scam-checklist/), and to vet one quickly, [How to find a legitimate 24-hour locksmith](/how-to-find-legitimate-24-hour-locksmith/). Typical prices for other jobs are on our [locksmith price list](/locksmith-cost-price-list/).

## So it doesn't happen again

- **Take the address off your key tag.** If you want lost keys returned, Tasmania Police and Victoria Police suggest a phone number rather than your name and address ([Tasmania Police](https://www.police.tas.gov.au/home-residential-safety-tips/), [Victoria Police](https://www.police.vic.gov.au/preventing-motor-vehicle-theft)).
- **Keep house keys off your work or school ID**, as JHU Public Safety advises ([JHU Public Safety](https://publicsafety.jhu.edu/resources-and-services/crime-prevention-education/)).
- **Leave a spare with someone you trust**, not under the mat ([Tasmania Police](https://www.police.tas.gov.au/home-residential-safety-tips/)).
- **Save a vetted locksmith's number in your phone now**, before you need one. The BBB suggests finding one before an emergency ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)).

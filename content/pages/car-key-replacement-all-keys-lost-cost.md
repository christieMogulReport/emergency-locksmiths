**Short version:** when you've lost **every** key, nobody can copy one, so a new key has to be cut from the car's code and **programmed to the car's immobilizer**. A mobile automotive locksmith can often do that **where the car is parked**; the alternative is a **tow to the dealer** and possibly a wait for parts. A 2026 trade survey of locksmiths puts the average **"all keys lost" charge at about $228** ([Locksmith Ledger](https://www.locksmithledger.com/home/article/55401654/results-are-in-2026-national-average-price-survey)), while dealer quotes gathered by AAA, Edmunds, and Kelley Blue Book run from **about $150 to $500 or more for the key alone, plus the tow** ([AAA](https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/what-to-do-when-you-lose-your-car-keys), [Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/car-maintenance/the-high-cost-of-losing-your-keys.html), [KBB](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/key-fob-replacement/)). Some cars, mostly newer European models, can only be done by a dealer. **Ask for the total price before anyone comes out**, and expect a legitimate locksmith to ask for your driver's licence and registration.

Every number below is from a published source, linked next to it. They are national or regional figures, not quotes for your car or city, so call to confirm. If you're stranded somewhere unsafe, or a child or pet is locked in the car, **call 911 first**.

## Lost vs. locked in: which problem do you have?

- **The key is inside the locked car.** That's a lockout, a quicker and cheaper job. See [locked keys in your car](/locked-keys-in-car-lockout-cost/) for costs and roadside coverage.
- **You still have one working key.** Making a spare from it is the cheap case. Most customer-programmable fobs need **two** working keys to add a third, according to Consumer Reports ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-maintenance/how-to-replace-your-car-key-fob-a4802255222/)).
- **Every key is gone** (lost, stolen, or the only key broke). That's an **"all keys lost"** job, and it's what this page covers. The Locksmith Ledger survey priced it at roughly two and a half times plain transponder-key programming ($228 vs. $88 on average) ([Locksmith Ledger](https://www.locksmithledger.com/home/article/55401654/results-are-in-2026-national-average-price-survey)).

## First: retrace, then gather what you'll need

1. **Search properly once.** AAA's advice is to check every pocket and bag, under the car, the driver's floor if the car is open, and then walk back through your day ([AAA](https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/what-to-do-when-you-lose-your-car-keys)). Call the last places you stopped.
2. **Work out what kind of key you had**: a plain metal key, a key with a chip (transponder), a remote "switchblade" key, or a push-to-start smart key. That decides who can help and the price (see the tables below).
3. **Gather your documents.** AAA lists the **VIN**, the **year, make and model**, and **registration, title or purchase paperwork** ([AAA](https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/what-to-do-when-you-lose-your-car-keys)). Dealers told Kelley Blue Book they won't program a key without proof of ownership ([KBB](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/key-fob-replacement/)). The VIN is usually visible through the windshield on the driver's side; your insurance card or app often has it too.
4. **Check your coverage before paying.** Consumer Reports suggests checking your warranty, insurance, roadside plan, or any key-fob protection the dealer sold you ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-maintenance/how-to-replace-your-car-key-fob-a4802255222/)). AAA notes your tow to the dealer may be free if you're a member and the dealer is within a certain distance ([AAA](https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/what-to-do-when-you-lose-your-car-keys)). Read the fine print: GEICO says its roadside service does **not** include replacement keys or fobs ([GEICO](https://www.geico.com/auto-insurance/emergency-road-service/)), and Progressive says that with its roadside add-on you pay only for the replacement keys ([Progressive](https://www.progressive.com/claims/roadside-assistance/)). Kelley Blue Book points out that a key claim on insurance counts against your deductible, so it may not be worth it ([KBB](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/key-fob-replacement/)).

**Sources disagree on how much locksmiths can do.** AAA's consumer guide says that for transponder and smart keys you'll need a tow to the dealer ([AAA](https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/what-to-do-when-you-lose-your-car-keys)). Edmunds and Consumer Reports say most auto locksmiths have the machines to program transponder keys, and the Locksmith Ledger survey shows locksmiths routinely pricing all-keys-lost jobs ([Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/car-maintenance/the-high-cost-of-losing-your-keys.html), [CR](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-maintenance/how-to-replace-your-car-key-fob-a4802255222/), [Locksmith Ledger](https://www.locksmithledger.com/home/article/55401654/results-are-in-2026-national-average-price-survey)). Our reading: it depends on your exact car and the locksmith's equipment, so **give two or three locksmiths your year, make, model and VIN and ask whether they can make a key for that car with no original**. If two say no, pay for the tow.

### When the dealer is the right call

- **Newer European cars.** Consumer Reports says European cars almost always need a dealer because their key encryption can only be programmed by the manufacturer at a few outlets; an Audi dealer told CR a fob "has to come from us" ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-maintenance/how-to-replace-your-car-key-fob-a4802255222/)).
- **Cars built within the last five years**, per Consumer Reports' chief mechanic, because of the expensive programming equipment needed ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-maintenance/how-to-replace-your-car-key-fob-a4802255222/)).
- **Anything still under warranty** where the key or immobilizer itself may be faulty: most new-car bumper-to-bumper warranties cover key-fob malfunctions, Consumer Reports says ([CR](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-maintenance/how-to-replace-your-car-key-fob-a4802255222/)).

## If the keys were stolen, have the old ones erased

A new key doesn't stop an old one from working. A Toyota service bulletin filed with NHTSA describes an immobilizer reset used when all keys are lost: once it runs, "all previously registered keys will be erased and can never be reused," and a separate procedure removes only the keys that are not present ([Toyota TSB via NHTSA](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2025/MC-11015495-0001.pdf)). Procedures differ by make, but the practical points are the same:

- **Ask whoever makes your new key to delete the lost keys from the car.** This matters most if the keys were stolen, or lost with your address or registration.
- **Bring every key you want to keep.** Under the Toyota procedure, any key not present can never be reused ([Toyota TSB via NHTSA](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2025/MC-11015495-0001.pdf)).
- **If your house keys were on the same ring**, see [lost your keys: should you rekey?](/lost-keys-should-you-rekey/)

## The ID check is a good sign (and how to avoid a car key scam)

Locksmiths get key codes and immobilizer data for many cars through the National Automotive Service Task Force's Vehicle Security Professional registry. Its rules require the locksmith to **verify the owner's identity with a U.S. or Canadian driver's licence** and **proof of ownership** (title, registration or similar) **in person, before** using that data, and in states that license locksmiths, registry members must show a locksmith licence ([NASTF terms](https://wp.nastf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/TCEdit-01082025v14.pdf), [NASTF customer D1 form](https://support.nastf.org/support/solutions/articles/43000756289-customer-d1-form), [NASTF locksmith licence](https://support.nastf.org/support/solutions/articles/43000562442-locksmith-license)). So:

- **A locksmith who asks for your licence and registration is doing it right.** One who will make a key for a car without asking for proof is a warning sign; dealers told Kelley Blue Book the same check protects you from thieves ([KBB](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/key-fob-replacement/)).
- **Ask for the total before dispatch:** trip fee, key, cutting, programming, deleting the old keys, and any after-hours charge. The BBB warns of phone quotes "as low as $15" that grow once the locksmith arrives ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)).
- **Get the business name.** Minnesota's attorney general warns about out-of-state companies posing as local locksmiths and says to avoid any company that won't give its legal name and location or answers with just "locksmith" ([Minnesota AG](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/consumer/publications/hiringalocksmith.asp)).
- **If the price at the car doesn't match the phone quote, you can say no.** Never sign a blank work order; get an itemized receipt and pay by card ([Minnesota AG](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/consumer/publications/hiringalocksmith.asp), [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)).
- **Ask before you agree what happens if the key can't be programmed.** If the locksmith lacks the equipment for your car (common with newer European models, per [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-maintenance/how-to-replace-your-car-key-fob-a4802255222/)), you shouldn't pay for a key that doesn't start it.
- **Be wary of cheap fobs bought online.** Kelley Blue Book says online sellers of programmed remotes may not be reputable unless they ask for proof of ownership, and Consumer Reports found some dealers won't program aftermarket fobs ([KBB](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/key-fob-replacement/), [CR](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-maintenance/how-to-replace-your-car-key-fob-a4802255222/)).

For every red flag in one place, see [How to tell if a locksmith is a scam](/how-to-tell-if-locksmith-is-scam-checklist/), and to vet a company quickly, [How to find a legitimate 24-hour locksmith](/how-to-find-legitimate-24-hour-locksmith/). Other job prices are on our [locksmith price list](/locksmith-cost-price-list/).

## So it doesn't happen again

- **Always keep two working keys.** Consumer Reports says to order a second key as soon as you're down to one ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/cars/car-maintenance/how-to-replace-your-car-key-fob-a4802255222/)). Copying a key you have costs far less than an all-keys-lost job ([Locksmith Ledger](https://www.locksmithledger.com/home/article/55401654/results-are-in-2026-national-average-price-survey)).
- **A cheap door-only key.** Edmunds suggests a basic key without the chip: it won't start the engine but will get you in if you lock the keys inside ([Edmunds](https://www.edmunds.com/car-maintenance/the-high-cost-of-losing-your-keys.html)).
- **A Bluetooth tracker on the ring**, and one fixed spot for keys at home, as AAA suggests ([AAA](https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/what-to-do-when-you-lose-your-car-keys)).
- **Save a vetted locksmith's number now.** The BBB suggests finding one before an emergency ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)).

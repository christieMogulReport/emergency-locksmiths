**Short version:** if you come home to a break-in, **don't go inside**. Get somewhere safe and **call 911** (or the police non-emergency line if the intruder is clearly gone). Once police have cleared the scene, **photograph everything before you touch it**, get the **report number**, and **call your insurer**. Then make the home lockable before the first night: **rekey** every lock the burglar may have a key for, and **replace** any lock that was forced or broken. Published guides put rekeying at roughly **$50 to $130 per lock**, an **emergency** locksmith call-out at **$130 to $240** before any work, and a typical door repair at about **$240**. Keep every receipt for your claim.

Every number below comes from a published source, linked next to it. They are national figures, not quotes for your city, so call to confirm.

## The first 24 hours, in order

SafeWise's advice for the first day is that it's "about order, not fixing everything" ([SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)). This is the order the sources agree on:

1. **Get safe first.** If a door is forced or something looks wrong, don't go in; the intruder may still be inside. Go to a neighbor's or your car and call the police, then wait for officers to clear the house ([SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)).
2. **Don't touch, clean, or fix anything yet.** That includes broken glass, damaged locks, and open drawers. Moving things can disturb evidence ([SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)). Yes, even the broken lock: a locksmith can wait until police are done.
3. **Get a police report.** The Insurance Information Institute says to report any burglary to police and get the report and the names of the officers you spoke with, since your insurer may ask for them ([III](https://www-legacy.iii.org/article/how-to-file-a-homeowners-claim)).
4. **Photograph and film, outside in.** Entry points first (forced locks, pry marks, broken windows, the garage door), then each room ([SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)).
5. **Save camera footage now.** Doorbell cameras, security cameras, smart-lock history, garage-door apps, and alarm logs can overwrite clips quickly ([SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)).
6. **Call your insurer.** III suggests asking: Am I covered? How long do I have to file? Will the claim be more than my deductible? Will I need repair estimates? ([III](https://www-legacy.iii.org/article/how-to-file-a-homeowners-claim)). SafeWise suggests starting the claim within the first day ([SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)).
7. **Secure the home before the first night.** Take photos first, then cover broken windows, repair damaged locks, or block any entry point that won't close. If the home can't be secured, stay somewhere else until it can ([SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)).

## Which locks to rekey and which to replace

Angi's advice is blunt: "If you experience a break-in or a house key has gone missing, rekey the locks immediately" ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm)). Rekeying changes the pins so old keys stop working; replacing swaps the whole lock. (Full comparison: [Rekey vs. replace locks](/rekey-vs-replace-locks-cost/).)

| What happened | Usually | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keys, a spare, or a bag with keys was **taken** | **Rekey every lock** those keys open | The burglar may have a working key. SafeWise lists rekeying first when keys are stolen ([SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)). More: [lost or stolen keys](/lost-keys-should-you-rekey/). |
| Entry with **no sign of force** (a hidden spare, an unlocked door, or unknown) | **Rekey** the exterior locks | You can't rule out a copied or found key; Angi says to rekey right after a break-in ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/should-you-replace-or-rekey-locks.htm)). |
| A lock was **forced, pried, or drilled** | **Replace that lock**, rekey the rest to match | A damaged lock is a replacement job, not a rekey ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)). |
| The **door or frame** is split and won't latch | **Door/frame repair**, then a new lock | A new lock can't hold in a broken jamb. See costs below. |
| **Garage remote** or **access codes** were taken | **Reset the opener, change smart-lock and alarm codes** | SafeWise lists resetting garage openers and updating smart-lock codes and alarm passwords ([SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)). |

**Check with your insurer before major repairs** or throwing away damaged parts. III says to make reasonable temporary repairs to prevent further damage, avoid discarding damaged items until the adjuster visits if you can, and **save receipts** because you may be able to submit them for reimbursement ([III](https://www-legacy.iii.org/article/how-to-file-a-homeowners-claim), [SafeWise](https://www.safewise.com/lifestyle/home-security-systems/what-to-do-in-24-hours-after-burglary/)). Keep the broken lock in a bag. Whether your policy covers lock or door repair is a policy question, so ask the claims line rather than assuming.

If a door was kicked in, the door or jamb repair can cost more than the locks. These are our readings of national ranges, not quotes.

## Tonight or tomorrow morning?

- **Call tonight** if a door or window won't lock, or if your keys or a spare were taken. A burglar holding your key is the one case where waiting makes no sense.
- **Tomorrow can be fine** if every door locks, nothing suggests a key was taken, and you feel safe staying (or can stay elsewhere). HomeGuide puts the emergency call-out at roughly double the normal one ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)).
- **Renters:** tell your landlord or property manager right away; they may need to approve, or handle, lock changes. Check your lease.
- **One visit, one trip fee:** ask the locksmith to rekey all exterior locks to one key in the same visit ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/cost-to-change-locks)).

## Don't get scammed on top of the break-in

You're stressed, it may be late, and you're searching "emergency locksmith near me" on your phone, which is exactly when bait-and-switch locksmiths do best. The BBB warns that scammers often claim a lock can't be picked and must be drilled and replaced with an expensive new one ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)). After a break-in you aren't locked out, so there's even less reason to drill anything that still works.

Before anyone starts, ask:

- "Can you **rekey** the locks that still work instead of replacing them?"
- "What's the **total**, including the trip fee, labor, parts, and after-hours charge?"
- "Will you give me an **itemized invoice** with your business name and address?" (You'll want it for the insurance claim.)

Then **pay by card**. For the full list of red flags, see [How to tell if a locksmith is a scam](/how-to-tell-if-locksmith-is-scam-checklist/), and to vet one fast, [How to find a legitimate 24-hour locksmith](/how-to-find-legitimate-24-hour-locksmith/).

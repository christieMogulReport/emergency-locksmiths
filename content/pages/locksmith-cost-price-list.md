**Short version:** most published price guides put a plain **house or car lockout at roughly $50 to $200 in normal hours**, with nights, weekends, and holidays adding **about $50 to $250 more**, depending on the source. **Rekeying** usually runs **$50 to $130 per lock** (or $100 to $300 per door once the trip fee is added), **replacing a lock** about **$80 to $550** depending on the lock, and a **car key** anywhere from **$70 to $400 or more** depending on whether it has a chip or is a push-to-start fob. A phone quote of $15 to $45 is not a bargain, it's the opening line of a well-known scam. Always ask for the **total, including the trip fee and any after-hours charge, before anyone drives out**.

Every number below comes from a published source, linked next to it. They are national figures, not quotes for your city, so call to confirm. If a child, a vulnerable adult, or a pet is locked inside a house or car, **call 911 first**.

**About the sources.** The Locksmith Ledger figures are averages from a trade magazine's reader survey of working locksmiths; the magazine says it is "not a scientific poll" and that prices are generally higher in big cities. Angi says its range comes from 16,505 projects reported by its pros. HomeGuide and Fixr are cost-guide sites; their main locksmith pages are older (2024 and early 2025), so treat them as a floor rather than a 2026 price. Kelley Blue Book's key figures are typical ranges, and its fob quotes came from dealerships; it says a locksmith might save you 10% to 25% versus a dealer.

**How to read it at 2 a.m.:** add the after-hours figures to the daytime ones. For a plain lockout at night, the sources above land roughly in the **$150 to $250** zone (Locksmith Ledger's after-hours average is $174), which matches what we found for [house lockouts at night](/locked-out-of-house-at-night-cost/) and [car lockouts](/locked-keys-in-car-lockout-cost/). That's our reading of the sources, not a guarantee for your city.

## What drives the price

- **Time of day and day of week.** Every source above charges more after hours, on weekends, and on holidays. HomeGuide adds that if a locksmith squeezes you into a full schedule, you may pay the emergency rate even during the day ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/locksmith-cost)).
- **The trip fee and how it's quoted.** Many locksmiths charge a set service fee to show up, then an hourly rate or a set price for the job, and some add mileage ([Locksmith Ledger](https://www.locksmithledger.com/home/article/55401654/results-are-in-2026-national-average-price-survey)). That's why a "$20 per lock" rekey and a "$200" rekey quote can both be honest: one leaves out the visit. Ask for the total.
- **Where you live.** Urban rates are usually higher; rural or out-of-area addresses can mean a bigger trip fee ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/locksmith-cost)).
- **The lock itself.** High-security, electronic, and smart locks cost more to open, rekey, or replace. Angi lists a standard deadbolt at $30 to $70 for the hardware and a high-security lock at $100 to $400 ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/keys-home-locksmith-assistance-and-costs.htm)).
- **Opening without damage vs. drilling.** You may pay more if the lock has to be drilled and replaced ([HomeGuide](https://homeguide.com/costs/locksmith-cost)). Drilling is sometimes needed, but it's also a known scam move (see below), so ask why and what the new lock costs *before* they start.
- **Your car's key type.** A plain metal key is cheap; a chip key needs programming; push-to-start fobs cost the most, and some newer or luxury models can only be done by a dealer ([Kelley Blue Book](https://www.kbb.com/car-advice/key-fob-replacement/), [Fixr](https://www.fixr.com/costs/locksmith-service)). Losing *every* key costs more than copying one you have ([Locksmith Ledger](https://www.locksmithledger.com/home/article/55401654/results-are-in-2026-national-average-price-survey)).
- **How many locks.** Rekeying a whole home (4 to 6 locks) runs about $200 to $500, per Angi, and doing them all in one visit spreads the trip fee ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/keys-home-locksmith-assistance-and-costs.htm)).

## The bait-and-switch: why a tiny phone quote costs more

The most common locksmith complaint isn't a high price, it's a **price that changes once the locksmith is at your door**. The BBB describes scammers who quote "as low as $15" on the phone, then find "reasons" to raise the price on site, so victims "often end up paying hundreds more" than they were told. Some insist the lock can't be picked and must be drilled and replaced with an expensive new one, which the BBB says is most likely a deception ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)). The FTC has warned that some "local" listings route to a call center in another city, that the locksmith may show up in an unmarked vehicle wanting "significantly more money" than quoted, and may take only cash ([FTC consumer alert, reproduced](https://rockymountainsecuritygroup.com/files/HIRE_A_LOCKSMITH.pdf)).

Use the price list above as your yardstick:

- **Way below the table is a red flag.** If every source puts a lockout at $50 or more before any night charge, and the trade survey's average service fee alone is $94, a $15 to $45 "lockout" quote is almost certainly just the trip fee, with labor to be added later.
- **Ask for the total, not "starts at."** "What is the total to open a standard front door [or a 2019 Honda Civic] tonight, including the trip fee, labor, mileage, and after-hours charge?"
- **Ask what would change it.** "What would make it cost more, and how much?" And: "Do you try to open it without drilling first?"
- **Get a real business name and street address,** and check it while you're on the phone. The FTC suggests confirming that the address actually belongs to that locksmith ([FTC alert](https://rockymountainsecuritygroup.com/files/HIRE_A_LOCKSMITH.pdf)). A generic "locksmith services" greeting is a warning sign ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/article/news-releases/22797-bbb-scam-alert-locked-out-dont-fall-for-a-locksmith-cons)).
- **Confirm the price again before work starts,** and don't sign a blank work order. If the price at the door doesn't match the phone, you can send them away (ask on the phone what you'd owe for the trip in that case).
- **Pay by credit card** and get an itemized invoice with the business name and address. The BBB suggests a card so you can dispute a charge.

For every check in one place, including a printable version for the fridge or glovebox, see [How to tell if a locksmith is a scam: the full checklist](/how-to-tell-if-locksmith-is-scam-checklist/).

## Ways to pay less (legitimately)

- **Check what you already have.** For car lockouts, roadside assistance from your auto club, insurer, or car maker may cover the call; the FTC suggests calling them first ([FTC alert](https://rockymountainsecuritygroup.com/files/HIRE_A_LOCKSMITH.pdf)). Our [car lockout page](/locked-keys-in-car-lockout-cost/) compares the main plans.
- **Wait for business hours if you safely can.** Rekeying after a lost key or a move usually isn't an emergency, and Angi suggests scheduling non-urgent work during regular hours and rekeying instead of replacing when your locks are in good shape ([Angi](https://www.angi.com/articles/keys-home-locksmith-assistance-and-costs.htm)).
- **Bundle jobs into one visit** so you pay one trip fee.
- **Pick a locksmith before you need one.** The FTC recommends researching a locksmith the way you would a plumber or electrician, and saving their details ([FTC alert](https://rockymountainsecuritygroup.com/files/HIRE_A_LOCKSMITH.pdf)).
- **Have a spare key.** Copying a car key you have is far cheaper than an all-keys-lost job ([Locksmith Ledger](https://www.locksmithledger.com/home/article/55401654/results-are-in-2026-national-average-price-survey), [Fixr](https://www.fixr.com/costs/locksmith-service)).
